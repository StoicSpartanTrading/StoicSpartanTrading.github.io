The Spartan Story

Before Trading: 18 Years of Behavioural Transformation

The experience behind Stoic Spartan — where the Spartan identity came from, the principles beneath it, and why understanding human behaviour matters when money and pressure enter the equation.

← HomeMeet MarkApply

Long before I worked with traders, I spent 18 years helping people change deeply ingrained patterns of behaviour that were evidently beyond their control, beyond their power to change on their own, and were having detrimental — and sometimes catastrophic — consequences on their lives.

Across rehabilitation settings, prison-based recovery programmes and community support, I worked with people whose alcoholism and addiction were often intertwined with trauma, depression, abuse, domestic violence, damaged relationships, criminality, shame and years of destructive coping.

During that time, I delivered thousands of group sessions, workshops, presentations and seminars, alongside thousands upon thousands of one-to-one sessions, mentoring conversations and individual interventions, with an incredible amount of success.

Motivational speaking was also an integral part of my work, particularly for those more resistant to change. I used it to inspire people, to generate an ever-increasing desire and commitment from them to do what was necessary to change themselves and their lives — for themselves.

I worked with an extraordinary variety of people: young, old, from different religions, different cultures and different backgrounds. Some were highly motivated to change. Others were confrontational, resistant, frightened, angry, ashamed, deeply mistrustful or convinced that meaningful change was impossible for them.

My job was to help them identify and overcome their personal barriers, to inspire them, motivate them, to clear the way for progress, and to metaphorically hold their hand and walk alongside them on their journey of improvement and inevitable success — sometimes despite the very parts of themselves that were standing in their way.

Outside formal rehabilitation settings, I helped establish and lead community support groups and recovery networks, supporting hundreds of people within their own communities.

Across those 18 years, I worked directly with more than 1,500 individuals. Every one of them was different, as were their circumstances, and many came from different countries. I was privileged to play a part in some of the most extraordinary and profound transformations imaginable.

The work was never simply about telling someone to stop drinking, stop using or make better choices.

A big part of the work was helping them understand why their behaviour, thought processes and belief systems existed in the first place.

What was it doing for the person?

What need was it meeting?

Relief? Escape? Control? Stimulation? Belonging? Protection? Numbness? A temporary way of avoiding pain?

We examined the underlying mechanisms, beliefs, thought processes, emotional triggers, coping strategies, impulsive responses and compulsive behaviours that kept destructive cycles alive.

Then we worked to replace them with healthier belief systems, more constructive ways of thinking, stronger coping strategies, personal responsibility, disciplined routines and behaviours capable of moving the individual toward freedom rather than further into self-destruction.

The aim was not merely to remove a substance or a behaviour.

It was to help people reclaim their lives and achieve their own personal freedom from the shackles that had imprisoned them for years — sometimes for most of their lives.

The Spartan Story

The Spartan Story

Over time, many people began referring to the community we were building as the Spartan Army, and some began calling me simply the Spartan — the person creating, building and leading the army towards freedom.

It was never about aggression.

It was about helping individuals win the battles they needed to win against the personal barriers and obstacles standing between them and the best lives they were capable of living and creating.

That required discipline. Resilience. Courage. Honesty. Personal responsibility. Humility. Willingness. Perseverance. Self-examination. Service.

And above all, the willingness to do what was necessary even when every instinct was telling them to do the opposite.

Those principles sit remarkably closely alongside both Stoic philosophy and many of the spiritual principles found throughout recovery traditions.

They were principles of self-mastery.

Learning to recognise what you could control. Accepting what you could not. Taking responsibility for your own actions. Examining yourself honestly. Choosing your response rather than becoming a prisoner to impulse. Doing what was right rather than what was immediately comfortable. And continuing to move forward even when the easier option was to return to old behaviour.

Those principles form part of the foundation of Stoic Spartan today.

Behaviour Under Pressure

The Same Battle. A Different Arena.

Trading is not the same as addiction, and the two should not be confused.

However, many of the behavioural mechanisms, emotional impulses and disruptions to judgment and decision-making can be remarkably similar.

A trader takes a loss. Emotion rises. The urge to act becomes stronger. The plan begins to lose its authority. The immediate need for relief, control, revenge, certainty or stimulation can become more powerful than the rational decision that was made only minutes earlier. Risk increases. Stops move. Trades are forced. Profits are taken too early. Size increases. A missed trade becomes FOMO. One loss becomes three. The trader goes on tilt.

There is a physiological component to this as well.

The amygdala plays an important role in threat detection and emotional reactivity. The prefrontal cortex is heavily involved in planning, inhibition, judgment and deliberate decision-making. Dopamine is involved in reward, motivation and reinforcement, while cortisol forms part of the body's stress response.

These systems do not operate independently. Under stress, uncertainty, reward anticipation or emotional escalation, they interact in ways that can make disciplined decision-making substantially harder.

The purpose of understanding this is not to turn traders into neuroscientists.

It is to help them recognise and understand what is happening before the reaction becomes the behaviour — or, in a trader's case, the next bad trade, blown account, huge loss or tilt episode.

Because knowing that you should not revenge trade is one thing.

Recognising the physiological, emotional and cognitive sequence that is pulling you toward the revenge trade — and having a process capable of interrupting it — is something entirely different.

The Holy Trinity In Practice

Beyond the Charts

Technical Analysis matters enormously.

We can learn market structure, Auction Market Theory, VWAP, Volume Profile, Order Flow, liquidity, institutional price action, Fair Value Gaps, candlestick formations, multi-timeframe analysis, continuation and reversal models and the many other components that can help a trader understand market behaviour.

We can build and refine strategies.

We can improve entries, invalidation, location, confirmation and execution.

We can structure Risk Management properly.

But none of it matters if the trader cannot execute what they know when pressure arrives.

That is why my work with traders isn't just learning charts alone.

We examine the trader's thought processes.

We identify emotional and impulsive responses.

We look at the mindset surrounding wins, losses, missed trades, drawdown, funding and money.

We examine what the emotion is causing the trader to feel, what the behaviour is trying to achieve, and what action that feeling is pulling them toward.

We challenge the story being told in the moment.

“I need to make it back.”
“This one is different.”
“I can't miss this move.”
“I was right — the market was wrong.”
“One more trade.”

Then we build rules, routines, risk controls, accountability structures and decision-making processes capable of standing between the impulse and the click.

Understanding the pattern is the beginning.

Being able to behave differently when money, uncertainty and pressure are present is where the transformation occurs.

Stoic Spartan

A Different Spartan Army

Years ago, the goal was to help people break free from their personal shackles that had imprisoned them for years — sometimes a lifetime.

Today, the arena is different, but the underlying principle remains remarkably similar.

Now we are building a different kind of Spartan Army.

Stoic Spartan.

Helping traders break free from the destructive patterns and detrimental cycles that repeatedly undermine their own potential:

Impulsivity. Fear. FOMO. Revenge trading. Overconfidence. Hesitation. Avoidance. Risk escalation. Inconsistency. Loss of discipline.

To understand themselves. To understand the market. To build structure. To develop control. To replace destructive patterns with deliberate ones.

And ultimately, to become the trader they hope to become, capable of doing what they said they had planned to do, even when the pressure of having live money on the line arrives.

That is the foundation of Stoic Spartan.
